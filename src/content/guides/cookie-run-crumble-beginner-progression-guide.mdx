Most early walls in CookieRun: Crumble are self-inflicted. The game throws ten upgrade systems at you, covers the screen in claim buttons, and quietly lets you spend premium currency on the least useful banner. Then a boss survives the 30-second timer with a sliver of health and your account feels broken.

It probably is not broken. Your power is sitting in unclaimed menus, or you spent materials on a system that pays back too slowly. Fix the route and the same account can move again.

<GuideSection id="protect-your-crystals" title="Protect your crystals from bad pulls">

Cookie and Pet pulls have separate Gacha levels. Every pull adds one EXP to the matching Gacha level, and higher levels add higher rarities to the pool. That means a pull gives you a unit or duplicate now and moves the account toward a better pool later.

Pet Gacha reaches its top listed rarity sooner. SSR Pets can appear from level 2, where the displayed SSR rate is 1.6%. Cookie Gacha takes longer. SSR Cookies enter the regular pool at level 4, while TSSR Cookies do not enter that pool until level 6. A special Oven Wanderer banner can give you access to a TSSR much earlier than the normal level gate.

The easy mistake is dumping crystals into Premium Cookie Gacha because it looks permanent and safe. Pickup banners are usually the better place for those crystals. Their pulls still count toward Cookie Gacha EXP, but their milestone track can also give extra rewards and copies of the featured Cookie. The base rates are not magically better. You simply get more back for crossing the same number of pull milestones.

Pet Gacha needs a different rule. Use Pet tickets from quests, missions, and check-ins. Keep crystals for Cookie pickup banners. Pet rates become generous early enough that spending premium currency there solves a problem you were likely to solve with free tickets anyway.

<GuideTip title="The short version">

Spend crystals on a Cookie pickup banner you are willing to commit to. Spend Pet tickets in Pet Gacha. Use the special currency from check-ins and the free pass track on the Oven Wanderer banner because crystals cannot replace that currency.

</GuideTip>

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="use-the-quest-loop" title="Use the quest loop until it stops paying">

The repeating quest chain can feel tedious, but it is the engine behind fast early progress. Its rewards feed Cookie pulls, Pet pulls, Bakery gear rolls, account EXP, and duplicates. Those pulls raise Gacha levels, which improve future pools. One boring quest can touch half the account.

Follow the chain instead of wandering between menus. When it asks for summons, use the tickets it just handed you. When it points to the Oven, stoke or roll the requested amount. The next quest often replaces enough of what you spent to keep the loop moving.

Do the daily and weekly missions before they reset. Open check-in rewards even on days when you do not plan to play. The free side of each pass also matters. It can hold regular summon currency and the special material used for the Oven Wanderer banner. You do not need to buy a pass to claim its free track.

Claim collection bonuses when new Cookies, Pets, or promotions add a notification. They include crystals and permanent stats. Leaving ten red dots untouched is like keeping cash under the doormat, except the doormat is also making your team weaker.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="level-the-oven" title="Level the Oven without gear panic">

The Oven has its own progress bar. Stoke it enough times and its level rises, which opens better gear rarities. Early gear is temporary by design, so treating every piece like an endgame project burns time for almost no return.

Excellent+ works at the very beginning. Move Auto to Magic+ once its appearance rate is practical. Pick the best tier sitting around a 10% rate or lower, then check the Oven when the quest loop brings you back.

Equip the piece with higher displayed power. Sell the weaker leftovers and feed the returned red currency back into Oven levels. Early Epic gear may have only one substat slot, so a perfect substat hunt barely has room to matter. Damage is nice when two pieces are close, but raw power is a better sorting tool during the first stretch.

Gear presets become useful when you own several developed PvP or specialized teams. Before that, one general set is easier to maintain. Presets full of half-finished equipment create work without clearing another stage.

<GuideTip title="When to care about substats">

Wait until higher rarity gear stays equipped for more than a few hours and gives you enough substat slots to build around. Early on, wear the bigger number, sell the smaller number, and keep the Oven moving.

</GuideTip>

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="push-dungeons" title="Push dungeons before farming old floors">

Daily Dungeon tickets refill, so capped tickets are lost progress. Clear each dungeon every day, but do not settle into an easy floor too early. New levels carry first-clear rewards, and milestone rewards add another layer on top. Every fifth level can pay summons. EXP stages also hand out a large burst when you push their first clears.

Coin Dungeon matters because its rewards feed Resolve upgrades. Other Daily Dungeons provide research materials and the account currencies that unlock permanent power. If the next floor is close, improve the team and try it. Repeating the comfortable floor first means giving up the exact first-clear reward that might have pushed the account forward.

Crumble Dungeon scores the wider roster rather than only your usual lineup. Keep the main team capped, then spread spare Cookie EXP across units that will join the attempt. Broad leveling pays back immediately here.

In Arena, set both attack and defense lineups. Spend attacks on opponents below your displayed power while the account is young. Losing one match is not a disaster, but allowing attempts to sit at the cap throws away Fame currency and rank rewards.

Plaque Tower is worth climbing until the next floor refuses to move. Floor 21 can secure Cheesecake Cookie if your pulls missed her, and later floors award useful SR Cookies such as Orange Cookie. A wall around floor 100 is normal for an early account. Take the rewards below it, improve elsewhere, and come back.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="build-permanent-power" title="Build permanent account power before blaming the team">

Resolve turns ordinary coins into stats that apply across the account. Put early points into attack, then fill defensive stats. Many campaign walls are timer failures, so more damage usually changes the result sooner than extra durability. If Cookies are dying well before the timer, adjust that order and buy the defense you are missing.

Arena feeds Fame, another permanent damage upgrade. This is why Arena attempts should not sit unused. You are building a stat layer that follows the account back into other modes.

Gnome Laboratory research works the same way. Follow the damage path while normal progress is cheap. For special research choices, research speed is a strong first pick because time becomes the gate before materials do. Extra Daily Dungeon entries are also useful, but a faster laboratory keeps every later project moving.

Stellar Links adds stats through constellation coverage. Shuffle until an early layout reaches roughly 60% area or better. Lock a strong section if the rest needs rerolling, but watch the rising cost. A perfect day-one board can consume currency that would have produced several good boards. Constellation points come from check-ins, pass rewards, and unlocked exchanges.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="move-cookies-in-combat" title="Move your Cookies when the timer is the enemy">

You can drag the whole Cookie group during combat with a finger or mouse. The team stops attacking while it moves, so random shuffling loses damage. One deliberate move can save far more time than it costs.

Bosses sometimes spawn away from your starting position or arrive with enemies spread around them. Drag the team into the middle of the pack as soon as the targets appear. Area attacks begin connecting with more enemies, short-range Cookies spend less time walking, and the 30-second timer starts producing damage instead of travel time.

Try this before spending another pile of upgrades on a stage that ends at 2% boss health. If the same lineup clears after one clean reposition, the account was strong enough. It was standing in the wrong place.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="build-a-lineup" title="Build a lineup that works with your account">

Keep the active lineup at its current level cap. Level reset is free while the reset screen shows no cost, so a new pull does not force you to abandon all the EXP invested in the old slot. Check the confirmation screen before every reset in case the rule changes, reclaim the materials, and move them to the replacement.

Start with one Defense Cookie. Move to two when the front line collapses, and use a third only when a mode keeps deleting the team. Two Supports is a dependable baseline. Attack buffs, defense reduction, or healing can add more than another mediocre attacker. Fill the rest with damage dealers that connect to your Synergy setup.

Hold a Cookie in the lineup screen to inspect its Synergy. Granted effects need matching receivers. Duration, for example, increases skill duration for allies that receive that Synergy. A row of impressive Granted icons does nothing for Cookies that cannot receive them. Use the [Team Builder](/tools/team-builder/) to check which matches become active before spending EXP on a replacement.

Sugar Runes unlock early. Put the main Rune effect on every Cookie in the working lineup before chasing ideal rolls. More slots open with promotion stars, so heavy rerolling makes more sense after those slots exist.

You do not need twelve SSR Cookies to start. Popcorn Cookie brings strong area damage. Orange Cookie adds CRIT Chance Up and grants Multi-strike. Cheesecake Cookie supplies ATK Up. Poison Mushroom Cookie is an accessible R option for area damage over time, while Blackberry Cookie and Dark Cherry Cookie can fill early damage slots. Browse their exact Role, Element, Buff, and Synergy data in the [Cookie index](/cookies/), then use the units your account can level now.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="pick-pets" title="Pick Pets for the problem you can see">

Damage Pets are the default because the timer causes more early failures than survival. CRIT damage and Element-specific bonuses become better when the lineup already leans into those stats. If a team uses several Fire Cookies, a Pet that buffs Fire allies has a real job. Adding it to a mixed roster because its rarity is high does not create the same payoff.

Sweet and Sour is a CRIT damage option tied to the referral menu. It accepts another player's MID once. Your own reward progresses when invited players clear Chapter 1, and five successful referrals can complete the duplicate track if that reward is still listed. Check the in-game panel because referral rewards can rotate.

Do not force damage if Cookies are dying. Swap one Pet for recovery or defense and run the same stage again. The searchable [Pets page](/pets/) includes full effect text, which makes this comparison much faster than opening every shelter portrait.

Visit the Shelter whenever it shows a claim notification. Pet levels, promotions, and collection bonuses are small alone, but ignoring all of them creates a surprisingly large stat hole.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="join-a-guild" title="Join a guild before you feel ready">

A guild gives you gift boxes, research buffs, a help system, and its own shop. Ask for help on long Gnome Laboratory timers. Return the favor when other members request it. This costs very little and cuts one of the slowest early gates.

Donate, but do not empty your crystal balance to prove that you are friendly. Normal play produces guild currency, and that currency is the part you care about. Sugar Flame is a strong first shop purchase while Oven levels are cheap. Its value drops once a single Oven level costs hundreds of thousands, so switch priorities instead of buying it forever.

Dungeon keys, Sugar Rune materials, constellation points, and random SSR boxes become reasonable later purchases. Buy what opens the next useful action. A key you can spend today beats a material for a system you have not developed.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="use-idle-rewards" title="Use idle rewards correctly when progress stops">

Offline Auto Hunt stores up to eight hours by default. Research can extend the cap. Claim before the timer fills, use the lucky rolls available each day, and convert the haul into levels, Resolve, gear, and research before attempting the wall again.

While you are online, the offline box does not appear to fill because income goes straight into your inventory. Watch the gold counter and item totals instead. The system is still working.

At some point, strategy stops fixing the numbers. The boss survives too long and every sensible upgrade is spent. Close the game. The idle system turns time away into stats for the next attempt. Logging off can be faster than rearranging a team that already makes sense.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="daily-route" title="A practical route for every login">

Use this order when the menus start feeling like chores:

1. Claim mail, check-ins, pass rewards, collection bonuses, Shelter notifications, and guild gifts.
2. Spend Daily Dungeon tickets while pushing the highest clearable floor.
3. Use Arena attempts before they cap and check the defense lineup.
4. Follow the repeating quest chain until it asks for progress you cannot make yet.
5. Spend Cookie pickup pulls and free Pet tickets, then claim new Gacha level rewards.
6. Equip higher power Oven gear, sell leftovers, and stoke the Oven.
7. Cap the main lineup, unlock missing Sugar Runes, and spend Resolve coins with the current wall in mind.
8. Check Laboratory research, Stellar Links, the guild shop, and Plaque Tower milestones.
9. Push campaign stages. Reposition the team manually when enemies spawn apart.
10. When the next wall is numerical, let Auto Hunt build the next upgrade batch.

This order keeps limited attempts moving and gives premium currency one job. It also tells you why the account is stuck. Fix the missing stat, ignored mode, weak formation, or bad position. Throwing crystals at all four turns a small wall into an expensive one.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="beginner-faq" title="CookieRun: Crumble beginner FAQ">

### Should beginners spend crystals on the regular Cookie banner?

Pickup banners are usually the better crystal target because their pull milestones can add rewards and extra copies. Pulls there still raise Cookie Gacha EXP. Save Pet pulls for tickets earned through quests and other free rewards.

### What should I upgrade first when I get stuck?

Raise the main lineup to its current cap, spend available Resolve coins with attack first, equip higher power Oven gear, unlock the main Sugar Rune on each active Cookie, and claim collection bonuses. Try the stage again before replacing half the team.

### Should I farm an easy Daily Dungeon stage?

Push the highest new stage you can clear before repeating an older one. First clears and milestone floors pay extra rewards, and every fifth level can add summons. Farm lower stages after the next push stops working.

### How many tanks and supports should a beginner team use?

Start with one Defense Cookie if the team survives comfortably. Move toward two when enemies delete the front line. Two Supports is a dependable baseline, while the remaining slots can carry damage and complete useful Synergy matches.

### Why do Auto Hunt rewards look empty while I am online?

Online Auto Hunt income goes directly into the inventory instead of building in the offline claim window. Offline rewards store for up to eight hours before research upgrades extend that cap.

</GuideSection>
