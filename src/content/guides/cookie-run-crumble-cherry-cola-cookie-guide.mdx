Cherry Cola Cookie looks like an answer to a very annoying Arena problem: Milk stays alive, keeps the enemy team going, and watches your damage disappear into another heal. A Cookie who charges toward the highest-ATK enemy sounds perfect for that matchup. Then the fight starts, Cherry Cola drops, and Milk is still standing.

**Five stars is a reasonable stopping point for Stellar Link progress. It does not guarantee a working Milk counter.** If Pinot Noir still needs seven stars, that is the stronger combat investment to finish before pushing Cherry Cola further. You can want the promotions without handing the new Cookie a permanent Arena slot.

<GuideSection id="pull-budget" title="Decide what you are buying before you pull">

The five-star recommendation comes from account growth. Stellar Link counts Cookie promotions, so progress there has value even if Cherry Cola spends some time on the bench. A newer account has more room to benefit from that progression, which can make additional promotions worth considering.

That still leaves a budget decision. Five stars is a target to weigh against your available Cutters and Crystals, not a reason to empty every reserve. Pick the stopping point before opening the banner. Once you reach it, check what the next promotion would cost and which existing Cookie would lose those resources.

One large pull session started with about 500,000 Crystals and 2,700 Cutters and still fell short of full promotion. That result is not an average cost calculation. It is a useful warning against assuming that a huge stash guarantees a finished Cookie. Rare bonus pulls can make the session feel successful while your intended promotion remains unfinished.

For combat priorities, compare the rest of your roster with the [Cookie tier list](/tier-list/). A promotion that helps account growth and a Cookie who improves today's Arena team can be different purchases. Decide which benefit you need before spending another batch of Crystals.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="milk-targeting" title="Why Cherry Cola goes after Milk">

Cherry Cola is an SSR Water Charge Cookie who receives Multi-strike and Multi-shot synergies. The attraction in Arena is the highest-ATK targeting: an enemy who builds Milk around ATK and stacks the relevant captain effects can put Milk at the top of that check.

That creates an opportunity to reach the healer instead of spending the whole fight hitting something else. But the skill does not select an enemy because their name is Milk. If another Cookie has the highest ATK, the targeting logic points elsewhere. Check the opponent's setup before blaming the skill for chasing the wrong unit.

The displayed skill interval in the initial assessment was 1.75 seconds, alongside a large damage multiplier. Those numbers explain the appeal of a fast burst attacker. They do not tell you how many attacks Cherry Cola will finish after diving into danger.

There is another ATK check on your own side. In the setup under review, Cherry Cola's ATK sat below Milk's, so effects that favored the highest-ATK ally did not automatically prioritize Cherry Cola. Adding the new attacker does not mean your existing buff package suddenly belongs to them. Watch who receives it.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="arena-survival" title="The first problem is staying alive">

Cherry Cola's early Arena weakness was painfully simple: the Cookie moved out alone and died too easily. Against a strong defense, the expected healer assassination became a quick loss of your own attacker. A short skill interval cannot help much after that.

The initial swap removed the SSR Royal Excellence version of Space Doughnut. It kept the team's defensive support, including Nameless Cake Hound. That is a useful starting principle for your own experiments: remove a flexible slot before cutting the protection that gives Cherry Cola a chance to act.

A later attempt used a more defensive Pet setup. It did not turn Milk into a reliable early kill. Defensive Pets can give Cherry Cola more time. The Cookie still has to deal enough damage to finish Milk before that protection runs out.

Start with an established formation from the [team lineup guide](/teams/) and make one swap. If you change the frontline, Pets, captain setup, and damage core together, you will have trouble telling whether Cherry Cola improved anything. A familiar team makes a disappointing result much easier to diagnose.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="kill-check" title="A win can hide a failed assassination">

Watch Milk's health during the opening exchange. Does Cherry Cola take a meaningful chunk out of it? Does the target recover? Does Cherry Cola survive long enough to finish? The final victory screen skips the detail you need to judge this particular slot.

In the early attempts, Milk kept surviving even when the overall fight looked winnable. Cherry Cola could fall while the remaining team still had enough damage to win. Calling that a successful counter gives the new Cookie credit for work the rest of the lineup did.

The promising case is narrower: enough offensive investment, with a favorable stat matchup, may let Cherry Cola finish Milk quickly. Treat that as a condition to test. The early results do not establish a reliable answer against equally strong or stronger defenses.

For a useful comparison, repeat the same opponent with your original lineup and the Cherry Cola swap. Keep the other settings fixed. Record whether Milk dies early and whether your team wins. If the original team wins just as comfortably, Cherry Cola has not yet earned the slot, even if the damage number looks respectable.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="rune-spending" title="Avoid expensive rune guesses">

There is not enough here to name a settled best Sugar Rune build. The initial assessment explicitly left that decision open, and the Arena attempts exposed a survival problem before they established an ideal damage setup. Spending heavily on a supposed perfect roll would get ahead of the evidence.

The short displayed skill interval makes Skill Haste a less obvious first expense. It does not prove that Haste has zero value. First check whether Cherry Cola lives to use repeated skills at all. If the Cookie dies during the opening dive, shaving time off later casts misses the immediate problem.

Use the gear you already own for the first comparison. Then change one part of the setup to address what happened in battle: an attacker who survives but cannot finish Milk needs a different adjustment from one who disappears immediately. Avoid paying for several speculative fixes at once.

Also check which ally receives ATK-based support effects after any gear changes. Raising Cherry Cola's stats can alter that order. You want to know whether an apparent improvement came from the gear itself or from moving an important buff away from another Cookie.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="pinot-priority" title="Finish Pinot Noir before chasing more stars">

If you need a firm investment order, prioritize seven-star Pinot Noir before taking Cherry Cola beyond the five-star Stellar Link plan. Pinot Noir was the more dependable investment in this comparison. Cherry Cola still needed a formation that could turn the targeting idea into repeatable kills.

That recommendation concerns which upgrade to finish first. It does not mean five-star Cherry Cola is useless, or that every account needs to stop at the same point forever. A newer roster may still gain useful account progress from more promotions. An Arena-focused account needs evidence that those extra stars fix its actual matchup.

Once Cherry Cola reaches your planned target, benching the Cookie is an acceptable result. Test again when your setup changes enough to address the failed dive. You do not have to keep a fragile attacker in the lineup just because the banner consumed a lot of Crystals.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="cherry-cola-faq" title="Cherry Cola pull and Arena FAQ">

### Should I stop Cherry Cola Cookie at five stars?

Five stars is a reasonable Stellar Link target. Reassess before going further, especially if Pinot Noir still needs seven stars. Extra promotions do not guarantee that Cherry Cola will survive an Arena dive or finish Milk.

### Does Cherry Cola always target Milk?

No. The relevant targeting rule checks the enemy with the highest ATK. Milk becomes the target when the opponent's ATK investment and captain setup put Milk first.

### What is the best Sugar Rune for Cherry Cola?

This early assessment does not establish a best Sugar Rune. Test with existing gear, watch survival and target damage, and avoid expensive rolls until you know what your lineup needs.

### Is Cherry Cola already a reliable Arena counter?

The early attempts do not support that claim. Milk survived several dives, and some wins came from the remaining team's damage after Cherry Cola fell. Judge the opening kill and the match result separately.
