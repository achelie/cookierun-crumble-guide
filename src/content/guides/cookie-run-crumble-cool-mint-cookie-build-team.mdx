Cool Mint Cookie can look like a bad investment for a very understandable reason: she fails in plain sight. She moves into melee range, starts her drill, gets knocked back or stunned, and loses the hit. Even when the skill lands, her summons can disappear before they earn their slot.

Cool Mint is a specialist who needs the right partners. The useful setup is a summon core built around Cool Mint Cookie, Licorice Cookie, and Witchberry Cookie. It is strongest when several enemies split across the field or when summoned bodies can waste enemy attacks. Force it into a hard single-target fight with constant area damage, and the same team can look awful again.

<GuideSection id="why-cool-mint-fails" title="Why Cool Mint feels weak alone">

Cool Mint is an SSR Water Charge Cookie. Her position puts her close enough to be interrupted while she attacks, and she does not become immune to crowd control during the drill. That creates two separate problems. The drill can miss, and her summons can die before their damage adds up.

A raw damage comparison can be misleading. In a clean attempt, Cool Mint may put up a great result. In the next run, knockback ruins the cast and the number falls apart. Build around repeatable uptime instead of judging her from one lucky damage screen.

The answer is not eleven healers. Give the enemies more targets, keep the summons active, and use fights where wide damage has something useful to hit.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="summon-core" title="The three-Cookie summon core">

- Cool Mint Cookie supplies the main summons, area pressure, and crowd control.
- Licorice Cookie adds another group of summons that can absorb attacks and share the pressure.
- Witchberry Cookie covers split enemies with large block attacks and becomes the main wide-damage threat.

Licorice is the piece that makes the idea click. His summons do respectable damage because they scale with ATK, but their bigger job is taking hits that would otherwise erase Cool Mint's summons. Bringing Licorice instead of Cool Mint gives you sturdier bait. Bringing both gives the enemy too many targets and leaves Cool Mint more time to deal damage.

Witchberry handles the enemies that refuse to stand together. Her blocks stay active for 5.5 seconds while the skill has a 4-second cooldown. She already has continuous coverage without Skill Haste. Extra Haste can create overlap, but it is not the first stat I would chase.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="synergy-providers" title="Activate Cool Mint's two synergies">

Cool Mint receives Multi-shot and Duration. The core feels incomplete when either one is missing, so reserve provider slots before filling the lineup with extra damage dealers.

Cheesecake Cookie, Herb Cookie, and Tiger Lily Cookie can provide Multi-shot. Cheesecake also brings ATK Up, while Herb adds healing. The choice is practical: use Cheesecake when the team survives and wants more damage, or Herb when Cool Mint keeps falling over.

Milk Cookie's Crunchy Strong Pediatrician provides Duration plus ATK Up and healing. That is an easy fit because longer summon uptime and better survival solve the same problem from different sides.

Witchberry receives Pierce, but I treat that as optional. Rockstar Cookie can provide it and brings a shield, yet the slot may be worth more as healing, defense, or focused boss damage. Finish Cool Mint's two synergies first. Add Pierce only when the team already stays alive.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="sugar-rune-stats" title="Best Sugar Rune stats">

ATK is the cleanest stat for Cool Mint and Licorice because their summons scale with it. Skill Haste is useful on Cool Mint when you want more summon cycles. If she dies before the second cast, stop pretending another attack roll will fix the run and keep a defensive line.

- Cool Mint Cookie: ATK%, Skill Amp, Skill Haste, then a survival stat if needed.
- Licorice Cookie: ATK% first, followed by Skill Amp or Skill Haste.
- Witchberry Cookie: ATK%, CRIT Chance, CRIT DMG, and Skill Amp.

Skill Haste is acceptable on Witchberry, especially if it makes the blocks overlap, but her base timing already works. I would keep a strong Haste roll rather than throw materials away. I would not lock it ahead of a good offensive stat.

A useful SR ATK roll beats an SSR line that does nothing for the Cookie. The letters look expensive. The fight does not care.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="best-content" title="Where this team earns its slots">

Rowdy Bikers is the best example. Three bosses split up and ride toward different corners, which can leave ordinary damage dealers swinging at empty space. Cool Mint and Licorice put extra bodies on the field, so the bikers stop moving long enough for Witchberry's wide blocks to connect. The core also works against Rowdy Truck and can handle the Twisted boss with the right support slots.

Plaque Tower gives the trio another good job. The summons appear late enough that enemies can group before the extra bodies arrive, then soak damage once the floor gets busy. That can reduce the amount of healing the lineup needs. Milk and Grapevine Cookie may be enough on floors where the summons catch most of the pressure.

PvP is less consistent, but the summons can bait enemy skills and keep melee targets busy. Cool Mint may not top the damage chart immediately. Longer fights give her more summon cycles, and that is when her total can pass much more obvious carries.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="bad-matchups" title="When to bench the summon core">

Frequent area damage is the cleanest counter. A boss that wipes every summon every few seconds removes the reason to run both Cool Mint and Licorice. Pure single-target fights can also favor Scorpion Cookie, Rye Cookie, or another focused attacker.

GingerBrave at higher boss stages exposes the problem. The opener can nearly remove the team, and the summons do not solve that by themselves. The setup can still win after defensive adjustments, but that does not make it the best answer. If Cool Mint dies before cycling, replace one greedy slot with Ion Cookie Robot, Herb Cookie, or another protector.

<GuideTip title="Use the matchup, not the hype">

Run the trio when targets split, waves stay crowded, or enemy skills can be wasted on summons. Bench it when frequent area damage deletes the whole gimmick.

</GuideTip>

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="quick-build" title="Quick Cool Mint build">

1. Lock Cool Mint, Licorice, and Witchberry as the core.
2. Add one Multi-shot provider and one Duration provider.
3. Keep Scorpion for focused boss damage when a slot is available.
4. Build ATK on Cool Mint and Licorice. Give Witchberry broad offensive stats.
5. Add healing or defense if Cool Mint cannot reach her second skill cycle.
6. Test on Rowdy Bikers, packed Tower floors, or PvP before spending more Rune materials.

<GuideSection id="cool-mint-faq" title="Cool Mint Cookie FAQ">

### What is the best Cool Mint Cookie team core?

Use Cool Mint Cookie, Licorice Cookie, and Witchberry Cookie. Licorice adds summons that absorb pressure, while Witchberry punishes split or grouped enemies with wide block damage.

### Which synergies does Cool Mint need?

Cool Mint receives Multi-shot and Duration. Use Cheesecake, Herb, or Tiger Lily for Multi-shot, then add Milk Cookie's Crunchy Strong Pediatrician for Duration.

### What are the best Sugar Rune stats for Cool Mint?

Start with ATK% because her summons scale with ATK. Skill Amp and Skill Haste are good follow-ups. Keep a defensive stat when she dies before getting another summon cycle.

### Is Skill Haste good on Witchberry Cookie?

It is usable, but her blocks already last 5.5 seconds on a 4-second cooldown. ATK, CRIT Chance, CRIT DMG, and Skill Amp usually deserve priority.

### Is Cool Mint Cookie good for every boss?

No. She performs best against split targets, crowded waves, and enemies that waste attacks on summons. Frequent area damage and strict single-target checks can make a focused damage team safer.
