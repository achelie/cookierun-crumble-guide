A green power arrow feels like permission to equip a new piece and move on. That works until your total climbs, the boss survives longer, and the supposedly stronger Cookie starts missing the effect that made the team work.

CookieRun: Crumble uses combat power as a real progression gate, so the number matters. It just does not tell the whole story. Accuracy and Evasion can add a surprising amount of displayed power. A support can gain power while losing Skill Haste. Scorpion Cookie can gain damage while landing Venom Sting less consistently. Those are upgrades on the roster screen and downgrades in the fight.

Treat combat power as the entry fee for a stage. After you pay it, every stat needs a job. The rules below cover which Oven gear to keep, which Sugar Rune results fit each role, and when another lock is about to chew through materials for no useful return.

<GuideSection id="check-the-failure" title="Check why the team failed before changing gear">

Run the stage once and watch the failure. Do not open the gear screen yet.

If the timer expires while the team is healthy, you need more damage, faster skill cycles, a reliable debuff, or enough power to escape a stage penalty. If the front line disappears in the opener, more Crit Damage on the back line will not fix it. That run needs Damage Reduction, defensive stats, healing, or a different formation.

Boss attempts also separate consistency from peak damage. A Scorpion build with Focus may clear more often because Venom Sting lands reliably. A full offense build may clear faster when every application connects, but it can need several restarts. I use the first for repeatable clears and the second when I only need one lucky win.

Change one thing, run again, and compare the same stage. If you replace gear, Pets, and three Cookies at once, you will have no idea which change mattered.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="stat-glossary" title="Know what every stat is buying">

ATK raises basic damage and can also affect healing or support output on Cookies whose skills scale with it. DEF reduces incoming damage, while HP gives the Cookie a larger health pool. Damage Reduction cuts incoming damage directly and becomes especially attractive when a tank is getting deleted before supports can act.

CRIT Chance controls how often critical hits occur. Going above 100% is not automatically wasted in Crumble because the critical damage bonus can apply more than once. CRIT DMG raises the payoff of those critical hits. CRIT Resistance works in the other direction and is far more interesting in PvP, where denying an enemy burst can decide the opener.

Accuracy and Evasion oppose each other. Evasion gives a chance to dodge, and Accuracy helps attacks connect through it. They can matter in the right matchup, but they are weak default targets for general story gear. Do not keep them only because the power total likes them.

Focus and Resistance are another pair. Resistance helps a target reject debuffs. Focus improves the attacker's chance to apply them. This matters for poison, defense reduction, and other effects that the team expects to be active. The exact Focus-versus-Resistance formula and boss Resistance values are not clear enough to justify a magic threshold, so judge it through repeated runs.

Skill Amp improves skills, not basic attacks. That can mean more damage, stronger healing, or better support output depending on the Cookie. Skill Haste reduces skill cooldowns. These two stats travel well because almost every useful Cookie depends on a skill.

Move Speed changes how quickly Cookies cross the field. Boss Damage is limited to bosses. Elemental Damage pays when your element has the favorable matchup. These can solve a specific stage, but they are not as safe as Skill Amp or Skill Haste on shared gear.

Status stacking has its own catch. When two versions of the same buff or debuff overlap, the strongest one applies. Piling several weaker DEF Down effects into the same window may do less than adding a different multiplier.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="power-breakpoints" title="Respect power breakpoints without worshipping power">

Story stages can reduce your damage when the team sits below the expected power. One late-story example makes the problem obvious: around 6 million power on Chapter 42-1 produced roughly 55% damage, 10 million restored normal damage, and 12 million reached about 120%. Treat those figures as an example of the system, not a target for every stage.

A mediocre high-power piece can beat a perfect low-power piece while you are under the breakpoint. Escaping a large stage penalty may be worth more than a tidy set of substats.

The rule changes once the team reaches normal damage. At that point, another chunk of Accuracy that inflates power may contribute less than Skill Haste, Skill Amp, ATK, or CRIT stats. Compare the actual clear, not the green number.

<GuideTip title="The keep-or-sell question">

Ask whether the piece crosses a power breakpoint or improves the Cookie's real job. If the answer to both is no, the green arrow is decoration.

</GuideTip>

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="judge-oven-gear" title="Judge Oven gear in 20 seconds">

Once the Oven can produce Special gear often enough to be practical, use Special or higher as the serious keep floor. Special is useful because two substat slots give a piece enough room to support a build. Lower gear can still fill an empty slot, but it should not consume much attention.

Use this order when a piece drops:

1. Check whether equipping it crosses a stage power breakpoint.
2. Read both substats and ask whether they help the Cookie's job.
3. Prefer Skill Amp and Skill Haste when the piece may move between Cookies.
4. Keep role stats such as ATK and CRIT for damage dealers, Damage Reduction for tanks, or Focus for a debuffer.
5. Reject a power increase built from two poor-fit stats unless you need that number immediately for the stage penalty.

Accuracy, Evasion, Resistance, and Move Speed are the easiest general pieces to overvalue. Focus sits in the middle. It is wasted on a Cookie with no meaningful debuff, yet extremely useful when one missed effect ruins the boss attempt.

Do not chase a flawless set for twelve Cookies at once. Build the team you use and keep flexible pieces. I would rather store a slightly lower-power Skill Haste piece than sell it for an Accuracy upgrade I cannot name a use for. The Oven will keep producing replacements.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="universal-stats" title="Keep Skill Amp and Skill Haste when you are unsure">

Skill Amp and Skill Haste are the closest thing to universal targets. A DPS Cookie gets stronger casts and more of them. A support cycles healing or buffs faster. A tank can refresh protection, control, or its own damage more often.

They are also easier to reuse. If a Cookie leaves the lineup, an odd Focus or CRIT Resistance piece may sit in storage. Skill-focused gear usually finds another owner immediately.

ATK and CRIT are still good on the right Cookie. Skill Amp and Skill Haste are simply safer when you do not yet know which team will carry the next ten chapters.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="support-runes" title="Build support Sugar Runes around repeated skills">

Supports are the cleanest place to spend serious Sugar Rune materials because their useful stat pool is narrow. Start with Skill Amp and Skill Haste. Add ATK when the Cookie's healing or support value scales with it.

Macaron Cookie and Pomegranate Cookie appear in enough teams to justify early investment. Milk Cookie's Crunchy Strong Pediatrician is another support worth considering when it is part of your regular formation. A Rune on a support used in story, bosses, and dungeons pays back more often than a perfect Rune on a specialist who leaves the bench once a week.

This is where I am willing to lock early. If a support rolls a strong Skill Amp or Skill Haste line, I keep it and continue. The remaining target is narrow enough that the extra cost has a clear purpose.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="dps-runes" title="Give DPS Cookies a wider roll pool">

Damage dealers can use more good stats than the three-lock limit comfortably covers. Skill Amp, Skill Haste, ATK, CRIT Chance, and CRIT DMG may all improve the same Cookie. Vampire Cookie is a straightforward example: damage stats have an obvious job, and there is little reason to force Focus onto the build.

Because the pool is broad, I leave the exact-stat filter empty on most DPS rolls. Preselecting three lines can make you pay escalating costs while the system rejects other strong outcomes. Take the first high-quality offensive result, then lock after the Rune has given you a reason.

The best mix depends on the kit and the current account. A Cookie with plenty of CRIT support may value CRIT DMG more. A short cooldown can make another chunk of Skill Haste less dramatic than Skill Amp. Test the result on the stage you care about.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="tank-runes" title="Let tanks choose between survival and damage">

Skill Amp and Skill Haste remain useful on tanks, but the third priority depends on why the tank is in the team. Choose Damage Reduction when the front line dies before the first support cycle. PvP makes that choice even easier because surviving the opener has immediate value.

Madeleine Cookie and Nameless Cake Hound can contribute real damage, so CRIT Chance or CRIT DMG is not nonsense on them. If they already survive, offensive Runes can help a timer wall. If they fold, the damage roll belongs in storage until the defensive problem is fixed.

Tanks have several acceptable results too. My safe default is Skill Amp, Skill Haste, and Damage Reduction, but I roll broadly before paying to lock that exact trio.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="focus-debuffers" title="Use Focus when consistency is worth more than a restart">

Scorpion Cookie and Devil Cookie are the clearest Focus cases. Scorpion wants Venom Sting applications to build quickly. Devil Cookie wants DEF Down to land before the damage window. A missed debuff can make an otherwise strong boss attempt look weak.

Focus lowers that frustration, but it is not mandatory. Boss fights can be restarted, and an offensive Scorpion build can win by landing a lucky sequence while carrying more ATK or Skill Amp. When I need one clear, I am perfectly willing to reset for that run.

Use Focus when you want repeatable farming, fewer resets, or a debuff that the whole rotation depends on. Use more offense when the team only needs one high-roll clear and you are willing to restart. Since the exact boss Resistance values are not exposed clearly, compare batches of attempts instead of declaring one run proof.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="plating-priority" title="Spend Plating upgrades on account-wide value">

Plating affects every Cookie, so I put the Skill Amp book first. The attack weapon and CRIT staff are sensible next choices when story and boss timers are the problem.

Do not spread materials evenly just because every icon has a level. Push the plate that improves the largest part of your roster, then bring the others up when their cost becomes efficient. A focused account bonus changes more fights than twelve tiny upgrades that nobody can feel.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="reroll-budget" title="Stop Rune locking from eating your account">

Early on, R or better effects with the right stat are usable. Move toward SR and SSR as your supply improves. SSR results are rare, and the same stat can roll at different strengths. A high roll on a Cookie you use everywhere deserves protection.

Every lock makes the next attempt more expensive. That cost is sensible when a support already has one excellent line and only needs a narrow second target. It is much harder to defend on a DPS Cookie that could accept five different stats.

Promotion-gated lower slots take longer to fill. When a rare, useful line appears there, think carefully before rerolling it. Do not lock a bad stat just because the letters say SSR, and do not burn the reserve polishing a temporary Cookie.

Set a budget before opening the crafting screen. Pick one main-team Cookie, decide which results count as a win, and stop after reaching the budget. The button is very good at making one more roll feel cheap.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="quick-build-sheet" title="Use this quick build sheet">

- General Oven gear: meet the current power breakpoint, then favor Skill Amp and Skill Haste.
- Supports: Skill Amp and Skill Haste first, with ATK when their support output uses it.
- DPS: Skill Amp, Skill Haste, ATK, CRIT Chance, and CRIT DMG. Roll broadly.
- Tanks: Skill Amp and Skill Haste, then Damage Reduction for survival or CRIT stats for damage-capable tanks.
- Debuffers: add Focus when consistency matters. Keep offensive alternatives for one-clear boss pushes.
- PvP: raise the value of Damage Reduction, CRIT Resistance, and matchup-specific defensive stats.

Use the [Cookie list](/cookies/) to check each Cookie's Role and buffs, compare tested formations on the [Teams page](/teams/), and place your own roster in the [Team Builder](/tools/team-builder/). The [tier list](/tier-list/) can help decide who deserves rare materials, but a lower-ranked Cookie used in five modes may still be the better account investment.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="gear-rune-faq" title="CookieRun: Crumble gear and Sugar Rune FAQ">

### What are the best general gear stats?

Skill Amp and Skill Haste are the safest general choices because every role relies on skills. ATK and CRIT stats are strong on damage dealers, while tanks and debuffers need more specific checks.

### Should I always equip the item with higher combat power?

Use higher power when it moves the team across a stage damage breakpoint. Once the penalty is gone, compare the substats and the actual clear. Accuracy or Evasion can raise power without fixing damage, survival, or debuff consistency.

### What should I lock on support Sugar Runes?

Lock strong Skill Amp and Skill Haste results on supports you use across several modes. Consider ATK when that Cookie's healing or support output scales with it. Stop locking when the rising cost threatens upgrades for the rest of the main team.

### Does Scorpion Cookie need Focus?

Focus makes Venom Sting more consistent against Resistance, which reduces failed boss attempts. A full offense build can still clear by restarting until the applications land. Pick consistency for farming and offense for a one-clear push.

### When is an SSR Sugar Rune worth keeping?

Keep it when the stat fits the Cookie, the roll is strong enough to matter, and the Cookie stays in your working teams. Rarity alone does not rescue a useless stat. A good SR can help more than an SSR line that does nothing for the build.

</GuideSection>
