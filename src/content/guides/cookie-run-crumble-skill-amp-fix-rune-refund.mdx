Skill Amp used to be the lazy answer to almost every Sugar Rune question. Damage dealer? Add Skill Amp. Healer? Add Skill Amp. Debuffer with one useful line of text? Sure, pile it on there too. The August 27 adjustment finally puts a fence around that stat, and several familiar builds no longer get the effect players rolled for.

The change does not make Skill Amp bad. It stops the stat from strengthening specific debuffs, and it prevents DEF Down from pushing an enemy below zero DEF. If that debuff was the reason you rolled Skill Amp, the Rune needs a new job.

<GuideSection id="what-changed" title="What the Skill Amp fix changes">

Two combat rules changed together. First, DEF Down can reduce a target to zero DEF, but it cannot create negative DEF anymore. Before the fix, stacking enough DEF Down made an enemy take extra damage after its defense was already gone. That worked in PvE and PvP, so teams could gain more from a debuffer than the skill text suggested.

Second, Skill Amp no longer strengthens debuffs that reduce a target's stats. ATK Down, DEF Down, Accuracy Down, and similar effects now use their stated value. The same restriction applies to the affected Damage Taken Down effects. Skill Amp still improves damage, healing, shields, and eligible buffs when the skill allows it. Read each skill line before rerolling.

<GuideTip title="Keep the Rune until you read the skill">

Do not strip Skill Amp from every Cookie. Check which part of the skill receives it. A healer or damage dealer may keep the full benefit while a pure stat debuffer gets almost nothing useful.

</GuideTip>

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="affected-cookies" title="The 11 Cookies that need a Rune check">

The announced Sugar Rune refund covers 11 Cookies whose old setups could lean on the affected effects:

- Devil Cookie, Pancake Cookie, and Dark Choco Cookie use DEF Down.
- GingerBrave and Onion Cookie use ATK Down.
- Cocoa Cookie, Angel Cookie, Ion Cookie Robot, and Nameless Cake Hound have affected defensive effects.
- Gumball Cookie and Vampire Cookie carry other stat reductions that no longer scale the old way.

That list is a reason to inspect builds, not a command to bench 11 Cookies. Dark Choco still brings DEF Down. Ion Cookie Robot is still a sturdy defender. Nameless Cake Hound still has a job when a Fire frontline fits the team. What changed is the return from Skill Amp and from stacking too much of the same reduction.

Before spending new materials, check where each Cookie sits on the current [CookieRun: Crumble Tier List](/tier-list/). A Cookie with several useful jobs can absorb a weaker interaction. A one-trick debuffer has a much harder argument.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="defense-floor" title="Stop paying for DEF below zero">

The zero DEF floor punishes overlap. If Dark Choco and Devil Cookie already remove all of a boss's defense, another DEF Down effect cannot push the damage higher through negative defense. The extra slot may be better spent on CRIT support, elemental damage, healing, or a Cookie who survives long enough to cast twice.

You will notice the change most on low-DEF targets and teams that stacked several reductions. High-DEF bosses can still give DEF Down plenty of room to work. Test the same stage with the same Pets, then watch the clear time rather than trusting the displayed Power number. If the run barely changes, your team was not living off negative DEF. If damage falls off a cliff, the old interaction was doing more work than the lineup admitted.

The [Accuracy and Focus guide](/guides/cookie-run-crumble-accuracy-focus-guide/) still matters here. Focus helps a debuff land. It does not make that debuff stronger, and it cannot move DEF below the new floor.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="devil-cookie-build" title="Devil Cookie loses the easiest build">

Devil Cookie takes the ugliest hit because DEF Down is the main reason to bring him. His personal damage is modest, and he is fragile enough that teams often spend another slot keeping him alive. Skill Amp used to improve the debuff that justified all that babysitting. It now gives a small damage gain that rarely pays for the Rune.

Move Devil toward survival first. HP, DEF, or Damage Reduction can keep him alive for the cast that matters. Skill Haste is worth testing when another application helps after the first debuff expires. Focus still has a purpose against resistant enemies. Full Skill Amp is difficult to defend unless a later balance change adds another eligible effect.

He is not automatically useless. A boss with enough DEF and low enough pressure can still make his reduction worthwhile. Replace him when he dies early or when another Cookie already reaches the DEF floor.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="tank-builds" title="Retune Ion and the defensive group carefully">

Ion Cookie Robot shows why a blanket Rune reset would be silly. Skill Amp may still help an eligible shield or another part of the skill, but it no longer improves the affected reduction. If the old build existed mainly to stretch that defensive effect, try a tank set or Skill Haste and compare survival across a full fight.

Cocoa, Angel, and Nameless Cake Hound deserve the same treatment. Read each effect separately. Keep Skill Amp when the remaining healing, shield, damage, or buff is worth the stat. Replace it when the blocked line was the whole point.

The [gear and Sugar Rune guide](/guides/cookie-run-crumble-gear-sugar-rune-stats-guide/) has the safer reroll order. Lock one strong line at a time. Trying to rebuild four defenders on refund day is a quick way to turn compensation into another shortage.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="pinot-noir-rule" title="Pinot Noir already follows the new rule">

Pinot Noir Cookie is not part of the refund group because the kit launched with the restriction in its text. Skill Amp does not increase Healing Received Down. That anti-heal effect is already strong enough to pressure healer teams at its printed value.

Skill Amp can still improve eligible parts of the kit, including the resistance buff, so the stat is not dead on Pinot Noir. It is simply worse if you rolled it only to chase stronger anti-heal. Focus can help the debuff land against resistance, while Skill Haste offers limited value because the important timers already have good coverage.

The updated [Pinot Noir build](/guides/cookie-run-crumble-pinot-noir-cookie-build/) separates those jobs so you can decide whether resistance support deserves the Rune slot.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="rune-refund" title="Use the September 1 refund once">

Sugar Rune reroll materials spent on the listed Cookies are scheduled for refund on September 1. That gives affected accounts room to rebuild without paying twice for a rule that was not clear before the fix.

Take a screenshot of each current set before changing it. Write down the replacement stat and the content where that Cookie plays. After the refund arrives, rebuild one high-use Cookie and test it before touching the next. Do not roll extra lines now just to chase compensation. Nothing about the refund makes a bad roll useful, and last-minute spending may not behave the way you hope.

Devil is the obvious first audit. Ion, Dark Choco, and Nameless Cake Hound come next when they appear in several presets. A Cookie sitting unused at level 20 can wait. The refund does not expire because the red dot looks impatient.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="rebuild-checklist" title="A practical team audit after the fix">

1. Mark every affected Cookie in your saved presets.
2. Check whether two or more Cookies stack DEF Down on the same target.
3. Run one familiar boss or stage before changing Runes and record the clear time.
4. Replace Skill Amp only where the blocked effect was the main reason for the build.
5. Repeat the same fight with the new set before rebuilding another Cookie.

Use the tested [CookieRun: Crumble Teams](/teams/) as a baseline when an old preset loses too much damage. Swap one slot at a time. Replacing Devil, changing three Pets, and rerolling Ion in the same test tells you nothing except that the result is different.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="skill-amp-faq" title="Skill Amp fix FAQ">

### Did CookieRun: Crumble nerf Skill Amp?

Skill Amp still boosts damage, healing, shields, and eligible buffs. It no longer strengthens debuffs that reduce target stats, and DEF Down cannot push a target below zero DEF. The stat is narrower, not useless.

### Which Cookies receive the Sugar Rune refund?

The refund group includes Devil, Pancake, Dark Choco, GingerBrave, Onion, Cocoa, Angel, Ion Cookie Robot, Nameless Cake Hound, Gumball, and Vampire Cookie.

### Is Devil Cookie still worth using?

Devil can still help against targets with enough DEF, but Skill Amp no longer improves his main debuff. Build enough survival to cast reliably, then test Focus or Skill Haste if the fight needs them.

### When is the Sugar Rune refund?

The announced refund is scheduled for September 1. It covers Sugar Rune reroll investment on the listed affected Cookies.

### Does Skill Amp improve Pinot Noir's anti-heal?

No. Healing Received Down ignores Skill Amp. The stat may still help eligible parts of Pinot Noir's kit, including the resistance buff, so judge it by the matchup rather than the anti-heal number.

</GuideSection>
