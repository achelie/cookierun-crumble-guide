A boss can survive with a sliver of health even when your rotation looks clean. You retry, move a Cookie, swap one support, and still lose to the same timer. Then you claim a day of rewards, buy a few small upgrades, and the fight suddenly looks easy.

That jump is not always the extra ATK printed on the upgrade. Story stages compare your team's Power with the stage's Recommended Power. Falling below it can reduce the damage your lineup deals. A small account upgrade may push the team into a better damage band, which is why a routine Resolve node can feel stronger than its stat line suggests.

<GuideSection id="why-power-walls-happen" title="Why a stage wall can appear overnight">

Hard bosses still care about mechanics. Rowdy Bikers rewards wide attacks because its targets split apart, while a single boss usually favors focused damage. Power sits underneath that matchup. Bring the right team too far below the stage requirement and the game can cut its output before your good composition gets a fair attempt.

Claiming a day of Auto Hunt rewards can change the fight even when the lineup stays fixed. Levels, Resolve, research, gear, and promotions raise the total together.

Do not treat every failed run as proof that twelve Cookies are wrong. First check whether the team is close on damage, badly under the Recommended Power, or dying to a mechanic. Those are three different problems.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="read-the-damage-penalty" title="Read Recommended Power as a damage check">

The game does not show the full damage curve in the stage screen, so the exact percentages should be treated as working estimates. The useful pattern is clear: being far under the requirement can cut damage much harder than the visible Power gap suggests.

Stage 10-1 provides a simple example. Its Recommended Power is about 135,000. At roughly 13,000 Power, or 10% of that target, the estimated output is only about 1% of normal damage. At 20% of the target, the estimate rises to roughly 15%. The curve is not a clean one-for-one ratio.

Going above the recommendation may also raise damage beyond the normal baseline. The observed estimate tops out near 120%, but there is no reason to overbuild every old stage just to chase that bonus. Your goal is a reliable clear, not bullying a chapter you already finished.

<GuideTip title="Use the numbers as a diagnosis">

The exact percentage matters less than the direction. If a healthy team times out while sitting well below Recommended Power, raise the total before rewriting the whole formation.

</GuideTip>

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="stage-35-10-example" title="What the 35-10 wall looks like">

Stage 35-10 asks for about 4.2 million Power. A lineup around 3.3 to 3.4 million sits near 80% of the recommendation, and the working estimate puts its damage around 75% of normal. That is enough to make a good team look strangely weak.

Suppose the boss survives with 10% health. Replacing the entire lineup may introduce worse synergies and lower levels. Raising the same team by a few hundred thousand Power can be the cleaner fix because it improves the stage multiplier while keeping the rotation that almost worked.

Claim the idle rewards, level the active twelve, buy an affordable Resolve node, and try again. A sharp change after those modest upgrades usually points to a better Power band, not a secret build.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="team-versus-power" title="Keep the team, then test the Power gap">

Raw Power cannot turn the wrong damage type into the right one. An AoE roster can still stall against a strict single-target timer, and a boss team can waste too much time on split waves. Start with a formation built for the fight. The [recommended Teams](/teams/) page covers story, boss, F2P, and farming setups, while the [Tier List](/tier-list/) helps decide which Cookies deserve scarce levels and promotions.

Once the basic matchup is correct, compare the Power gap. A higher-Power Cookie can be worth a temporary slot when the team is just below a useful threshold. Check what you lose before confirming the swap. Breaking Multi-strike, Duration, healing, or the only defensive slot can cost more than the extra number gives back.

Use the [Team Builder](/tools/team-builder/) to lay out all twelve positions and three Pets before spending materials. It is much cheaper to spot a missing synergy there than after upgrading a replacement Cookie.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="cheap-power-upgrades" title="Cross the next breakpoint without wasting materials">

Start with upgrades that help the whole active lineup or cost very little:

1. Claim Auto Hunt and quest rewards before testing the wall again.
2. Bring the twelve Cookies you use to the current practical level cap.
3. Spend cheap Resolve coins, with ATK first when the timer is the problem.
4. Finish short Gnome Laboratory nodes instead of saving every material for one expensive project.
5. Equip unused higher-Power gear long enough to test whether the stage multiplier changes.
6. Promote Cookies when the next star adds a real skill upgrade or a large stat jump.

Resolve is easy to underestimate. A node may add only a small amount of ATK to each Cookie, yet it also raises the team total. Cheap research works the same way. Finish the short upgrades, clear more stages, and let the new rewards fund the slow project.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="gear-power-tradeoff" title="Use high-Power gear without worshipping the green arrow">

Equipment stats do not contribute equally to displayed Power. Accuracy can add a surprisingly large amount, so an Accuracy-heavy piece may push the lineup across a stage threshold even when that Cookie does not need more Accuracy for the fight.

That does not make the piece universally better. Under a harsh stage penalty, the higher total may win because it restores part of your damage. After the team reaches a workable multiplier, ATK, CRIT, Skill Amp, Skill Haste, Focus, or defensive stats may produce the cleaner clear. Test the fight instead of letting one green arrow make the decision.

The [gear and Sugar Rune guide](/guides/cookie-run-crumble-gear-sugar-rune-stats-guide/) explains the broader stat tradeoffs. If skills miss or debuffs fail after Power is high enough, use the [Accuracy and Focus guide](/guides/cookie-run-crumble-accuracy-focus-guide/) to fix that separate check.

PvP should be judged on its own results. The stage penalty discussed here is a PvE progression issue. Do not gut a working Arena build simply because another piece displays more Power.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="f2p-power-walls" title="Plan around the F2P slowdown">

Free accounts will feel the gap more as stage requirements climb. Paid memberships speed up AFK income and add dungeon attempts, so falling away from an early leaderboard position does not mean your account is broken.

Plan around daily progress instead. Free summons will fill more of the roster, duplicate copies will improve promotions, and some SR Cookies become excellent at five stars. The [beginner progression guide](/guides/cookie-run-crumble-beginner-progression-guide/) has a daily route for turning idle rewards into permanent account gains.

If a wall lasts one evening, that is normal. If it lasts several days, check the failure instead of waiting blindly. A timer loss, a dead frontline, and a resisted debuff need different fixes.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="power-failure-checklist" title="Use this checklist before the next retry">

- The team survives but times out: compare your Power with the recommendation and add focused damage.
- The boss ends below 10% health: chase the cheapest nearby Power breakpoint before changing the core.
- The frontline dies early: add defense, healing, or a better position. More damage will not protect a dead carry.
- Important skills miss: check Accuracy. If debuffs land inconsistently, check Focus.
- Split targets waste your attacks: switch to an AoE formation.
- One boss absorbs everything: replace broad wave damage with single-target pressure.

Run one controlled test after each change. Swapping four Cookies, three Pets, and half the gear at once only tells you that something changed. It does not tell you which upgrade solved the stage.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="power-guide-faq" title="CookieRun: Crumble Power FAQ">

### Does Power reduce my damage in story stages?

Falling below a stage's Recommended Power can reduce the damage your lineup deals. The exact curve is not shown clearly in-game, so use combat results and the size of the Power gap as your diagnosis.

### Should I choose Power or a better team composition?

Use the right composition first, then raise its Power. Boss mechanics still decide whether you need AoE, single-target damage, healing, or defense. Power helps that team operate without a severe stage penalty.

### Why did a tiny Resolve upgrade make the boss much easier?

The Resolve stats helped, but the extra team Power may also have crossed a better damage band. That can make a small upgrade feel much larger than the printed ATK increase.

### Is Accuracy gear worth using only for Power?

It can be worth a temporary test when you are just below a stage threshold. Once the team reaches a workable multiplier, compare the actual clear and return to stats that fit the Cookie if the Accuracy is unnecessary.

### Can F2P players get past late Power walls?

Yes, but the pace slows. Claim idle rewards, push dungeon first clears, finish cheap research, spend Resolve, and promote the main lineup. Treat a short wall as an account-building day rather than a reason to empty every resource into one Cookie.

</GuideSection>
