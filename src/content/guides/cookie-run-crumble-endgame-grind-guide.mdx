You change a Cookie, wait through another stage attempt, and still cannot tell whether the swap helped. Meanwhile, Arena tickets pile up and the next level eats millions of EXP. CookieRun: Crumble can ask for a surprising amount of attention once your account gets past the easy upgrades.

I would put faster retries and cheaper team experiments ahead of another long upgrade track. Battle reports could explain a failed swap; level synchronization and saved Sugar Rune builds could make the next one less work.

You can reduce some of that friction now by keeping a stable core and testing one change at a time. Other problems need changes to the game itself.

<GuideTip title="Suggestions, not an update announcement">

Level synchronization, summon wishlists, elemental towers, and the other proposed features below are design ideas. This guide does not announce a release date or confirm that the developers plan to add them.

</GuideTip>

<GuideSection id="slow-stage-attempts" title="The wait between useful attempts adds up">

A push from around stage 140 to 171 took roughly 15 hours in one account's experience. That is an example of the time commitment, not a clear-time target. Your roster and upgrades will change the pace.

At higher stages, leaving auto-battle running does not solve every wall. Around the 160 range, repeated lineup adjustments can become part of the push. Each failed experiment feels worse when you must wait before reaching the boss again.

A manual boss shortcut would help. Players could still choose when to attempt the fight, but would spend less time filling the gauge between meaningful decisions. Faster progression or a 2x option would also address the waiting. Neither idea needs to make the boss weaker.

Until your available controls solve that problem, give each attempt a purpose. Are you checking whether a tank survives the opener, whether poison stacks land, or whether the timer expires with everyone alive? Pick one. Compare an appropriate [recommended team](/teams/) with your own lineup, then change the slot connected to that failure.

If nothing changed since the last several losses, another identical attempt may tell you very little. Waiting for an upgrade is sometimes more sensible than watching the same loss again.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="battle-feedback" title="A damage report would make failed runs useful">

Skill descriptions cannot show how a Cookie moves through an actual fight. Target selection and time spent reaching enemies can make an impressive kit perform worse than its numbers suggest. Oven Wanderer is a useful example of the concern: a strong-looking kit still needs practical testing.

PvE battle reports should show damage dealt, healing, and damage taken. They would help in ordinary boss attempts, Guild Conquest, and Crumble Dungeon. A single-target fight asks different questions from a 12-Cookie Arena battle, so an Arena result cannot settle every PvE choice.

A report would not explain everything either. Low personal damage does not make a buffer useless. You would still compare the team's result with and without that Cookie. But actual battle totals would give you a better starting point than guessing from the power display.

For now, record what you can see: boss HP left, survival time, missed attacks, and important debuffs. Keep the stage and Pets unchanged during a comparison. That will not replace a proper meter, but it makes the next decision less arbitrary.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="exp-and-build-swaps" title="Level 90 makes casual experiments expensive">

The EXP example is stark: a level 97 Scorpion needed about 10 million EXP for the next level, compared with roughly 2 to 2.5 million near level 88. Those figures describe the example account, not every entry in an EXP table. They explain why raising several alternatives can feel slow.

A proposed level-sync system could use the lowest level among 12 invested Cookies. If the lowest were level 88, eligible lower-level Cookies could temporarily match 88. That would let players test a new formation without funding every candidate separately. This is a suggested rule, not an existing unlock instruction.

Resetting levels can help move investment, but repeatedly resetting and rebuilding takes time. It can also leave your Arena defense weaker if it uses a Cookie you just reset. Check that lineup before finishing the session.

Keep most of your EXP in the Cookies you regularly field. Use the [level 100 EXP priority guide](/guides/cookie-run-crumble-level-100-exp-priority-guide/) when choosing the next recipient, rather than leveling every possible replacement equally.

Sugar Rune presets would solve a related problem. A Cookie might need different stats for PvP and a boss, yet testing both should not mean repeatedly rebuilding the same setup. Saved builds would make those comparisons easier. Equipment presets and proposed Rune presets are separate features; do not assume one provides the other.

Until you have a switching option that suits the job, keep a note of your working rolls before experimenting. The [gear and Sugar Rune guide](/guides/cookie-run-crumble-gear-sugar-rune-stats-guide/) can help narrow the stats worth testing so you make fewer speculative changes.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="mileage-and-wishlists" title="Duplicates need somewhere useful to go">

Mileage can lose its appeal when you already bought the items you want and must wait for a shop reset. Extra tickets or keys do not solve that for an account that already struggles to use its existing supply.

A useful shop refresh would give those accounts more choices. That does not make every random SSR purchase worthless. A newer roster may still benefit, while an account with many promoted SSR Cookies may get little from another random copy. Check your inventory before treating a cheap item as a good purchase.

A summon wishlist would address the other side of that problem: getting the Cookie you need. Pomegranate, Scorpion, and Milk are examples of targets that could help a developing account. A wishlist could give players more direction as the pool grows, but its value would depend on the actual rules and rates.

Use the [Cookie Tier List](/tier-list/) to identify useful targets in your roster planning. That is different from assuming a wishlist will arrive soon or guarantee those targets. Do not plan a spending decision around an unannounced system.

Underperforming Cookies also deserve attention. Cream Puff is one damage-role example worth examining in balance discussions. More buffs could create useful alternatives, rather than leave players collecting Cookies they rarely bring to battle.

An awakening system or more promotion levels might give developed accounts another goal. I would still want better uses for the roster alongside it. Extending the grind without changing the choices could leave the same boredom waiting a little further ahead.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="better-modes" title="New modes should give neglected Cookies a job">

Element-restricted towers could make players build beyond their usual formation. An account that normally picks by skill strength would have a reason to examine the rest of its Grass, Water, or Fire roster. The restriction should create interesting choices, not just demand another expensive team before you can participate.

A roguelike PvE mode could offer temporary upgrades between fights. One run might favor an element; another might improve a class or attack style. A proposed bonus such as one second of invulnerability after a Charge Cookie uses a skill could change how you approach a wave. It is a design example, not a Cookie ability to build around today.

A run like that could work as solo or guild content, with rewards for the fights you clear. I would rather try a strange temporary build than repeat a familiar encounter with a larger HP bar.

Arena needs attention to time costs too. The feedback describes long fights before Pinot Noir reduced the effectiveness of healing-heavy teams, plus delays while choosing opponents and loading battles. Faster fights help, but an optional sweep could reduce the routine further. Players testing a lineup should still have a way to watch it fight.

Guild competition or another PvP format could add variety. It should offer a reason to make different decisions, rather than simply add another queue of tickets to clear. None of these ideas establishes a release schedule, and empty menu space does not confirm future content.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="shorter-session" title="Give the next session a stopping point">

You do not need to fix the entire account in one sitting. A smaller plan makes it easier to tell whether the time helped:

1. Pick one wall and note what ends the attempt.
2. Test one relevant lineup or stat change with the rest unchanged.
3. Check Arena defense after moving levels around.
4. Stop repeating the test when you need more EXP or another upgrade to learn anything new.

Keep Mileage purchases tied to something you will use. Leave hypothetical towers and awakening systems out of today's investment plan. If you have a working team and no useful adjustment left to test, collect what you need and come back later.

</GuideSection>

<GuideSection id="endgame-faq" title="Late-game progression FAQ">

### Does this guide confirm level synchronization or a wishlist?

No. Both are proposed improvements, not confirmed additions. The level 88 synchronization example illustrates a possible design rather than a feature you can unlock.

### Should I reset Cookies whenever I get stuck?

Only when you have a specific replacement to test. Repeated resets add work and can weaken your Arena defense. Keep a stable core and check affected lineups after moving levels.

### Should I buy random SSR Cookies with Mileage?

Judge the purchase against your roster. A developing account may benefit more than one with many promoted SSR Cookies. Extra stock is not automatically useful just because it is cheap.

</GuideSection>
